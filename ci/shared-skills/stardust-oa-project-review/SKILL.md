# Stardust OA Project Review

Use together with `$dingtalk-oa-approval` for Stardust approvals whose primary business decision is project initiation, a first-curve project, POC/new-opportunity investment, or a project special approval. This Skill supplies the project criteria; the generic Skill supplies evidence handling, scoring and the decision table that turns a result into an action. The rules are in this Skill — do not read `钉钉审批审阅原则.md` or any other reference document for them.

## Scope

Classify from the live process definition and form content, not title alone. A project can also trigger a separate finance, contract, or access/change review; load each relevant business Skill independently. This Skill does not supply contract-signing authority.

The company project criteria are supplied by this Skill. Read the applicable, task-bound revision before assessing rule coverage; an initial prompt or triage Skill that omits the project rules is not evidence that the applicant failed to provide company policy. Ask the applicant for project facts they own, not for the company's approval threshold or approval principles. If the selected Skill cannot be read, report that source-reading problem separately from an applicant material gap.

Project-governance documents (`跨部门协同服务标准` v0.2 / v0.3, strategy and operating-model notes) may be read as evidence of what was agreed for a specific project — scope, gates, owners, commitments. They are not a source of numeric approval thresholds, and their examples are not limits.

## Review checklist

Read the complete application, comments, attachments and linked project material. Record evidence for:

- customer/problem and strategic fit;
- project classification from the current application, including whether this project is explicitly first-curve, POC or special approval; a historical trial or POC row does not by itself classify the project now under review;
- a financial basis ledger before the margin decision: record the current decision unit and period, revenue, the source field designated as comprehensive/total cost, each component's inclusion in that total, and any historical trial or alternative scenario separately. Check the source field definitions and notes; do not obtain a project cost by summing every visible cost row. When a confirmed total already covers the current project, use that total directly and show component amounts (including “other costs”) only as its breakdown, never as an extra subtraction. Sum non-overlapping components only when no total is supplied and the component set is confirmed complete for the same scope and period. If the total's meaning, component inclusion or row identity is unclear, seek the source owner's specific explanation and calculation basis instead of choosing a convenient sum. A blank POC-demand field does not establish either a POC requirement or its absence;
- BRD or equivalent scope, effort estimate, staffing/resources and opportunity cost;
- customer confirmation, contract/budget status, delivery boundary, acceptance criteria and measurable outcome;
- cost ceiling, owner, milestones, dependencies, risks and mitigations;
- for a below-floor project: assess the strategic, customer, algorithm-efficiency, product-upgrade, delivery-capability and reusable value from the application and linked evidence, whether or not the applicant explicitly requested special approval; if the case merits an exception, identify the responsible manager's position and the applicable approval authority.

### Cross-source scope and schedule reconciliation

For every material customer commitment, compare the exact deliverable and acceptance boundary across the application, customer evidence, quote or contract, implementation plan, and milestones. Reconcile quantities or ranges, included and excluded coverage, sampling limits, delivery and acceptance terms, and dates or durations. A customer's confirmation of price, launch, or general commercial terms does not by itself prove acceptance of a technical scope limitation or resolve a conflicting delivery estimate.

If those sources materially disagree, identify each specific discrepancy and whether the applicant can resolve it with an existing customer agreement, a corrected plan, or a clear delivery/settlement explanation. Ask only for the unresolved, applicant-controllable facts; do not repeat facts already confirmed or ask the applicant to recreate evidence that is already readable. Keep a fact mismatch distinct from a policy gap, and do not infer misconduct or reject solely because the evidence needs reconciliation.

Keep applicant assertions separate from independently verified records. An item that was submitted but could not be read is handled under the generic Skill's principle 5 (unread is not missing).

Scale the review to the project (Derek, 2026-10-22). The checklist above is the full set for a large, hard-to-reverse or below-floor project. For a small, low-risk, reversible one, judged from the quote, cost and exposure shown in the form, check strategic fit and the margin rule, and ask at most one question that would change the decision. Differences that do not change revenue, cost, margin or scope (how the contracting entity or a document is named, formatting, a screenshot versus a table) are not grounds for a return; mention them in the approval remark when useful. A cost, margin or scope inconsistency is still a real gap.

### Known unknowns and reviewer-side read failures

- Read the latest applicant comments and prior review history before asking for project facts. If the applicant has explicitly said a fact is not yet available, record it as a **known unknown** with its source and date; do not ask for the same fact again as though it were omitted. If the applicant says a material project fact is not yet formed or is waiting for technical discussion, but could become available through coordination with the customer, technical team or another responsible source, formally return the application for coordination and resubmission when ready. Pending discussion is not proof the facts cannot be completed. Do not turn that material gap into a task for Derek to choose whether this incomplete instance may continue.
- Before proposing any return or comment, build a short issue-by-issue reconciliation from the latest review request to the latest applicant response: mark each item resolved, still unknown, or newly raised. A fact already stated as unavailable is not an unanswered request. Do not ask for it again in different words, ask for an unattainable document as if it were missing, or send a message that only repeats prior questions. Ask only for a genuinely new fact the applicant can supply. For example, when the applicant has already said that the customer has not set a POC budget or written quantitative acceptance criteria and that sample data is not ready, preserve those as known unknowns and assess their materiality; do not reopen the same three requests as though the applicant did not answer.
- Do not turn a completed response into another broad checklist. When the applicant has supplied a detailed point-by-point response, carry forward resolved points as evidence and ask only about a new, specific, applicant-controllable gap. Do not repeat the full prior return with “请补充/无法提供请说明” added to the end. If the applicant has said a material fact is not ready but can be completed by coordinating with its source, do not restate the broad checklist as unanswered; formally return the application for coordination and resubmission.
- For each material known unknown, identify whether an existing project rule makes the fact necessary to assess this exact decision. If the applicant or responsible source owner has a feasible path to produce a missing material fact, handle the current stage as a return for completion before considering any `needs_human` rule decision. Do not infer that the unknown is acceptable, create a new hard gate, or ask Derek to choose whether this incomplete instance may continue. After resubmission, if the facts are complete but a genuine company-rule, exception or authority gap remains, keep `rule_coverage < 1.0` and route only that separate reusable rule decision to `needs_human`. If the applicant and responsible source owner are both confirmed unable to complete the material and no feasible substitute exists, assess the known unknown under the applicable rule; the fact gap alone still does not justify asking the user to create policy.
- Separate applicant-side missing information from reviewer-side read failures. A denied link, inaccessible attachment, provider/API error, credential or local read failure, or exhausted retry budget means the review source could not be verified; it does not mean the applicant failed to submit it. After the same source returns a stable access or permission error, stop repeating the same read path; use another already-authorized source only if one exists, otherwise report the blocker. Do not use that failure alone as a return or refusal reason. Keep the approval pending when the unread source is material, and state the exact source and access/read error for follow-up.
- When one source is unreadable, continue assessing the independently readable evidence where possible, but do not represent the whole application as fully reviewed or approve based on an unverified material source.

## Project rules

1. **Strategic fit.** A project must fit the company strategy or bring a product, algorithm or delivery-efficiency upgrade. Check this first; it is the reason a project exists.
2. **First-curve margin floor: 25%.** A project explicitly classified as first-curve needs a comprehensive gross margin of at least 25%. Apply the floor only after the financial basis ledger establishes the current-project scope and period: `(current-project revenue − current-project comprehensive total cost) / current-project revenue`. Show the chosen source fields, subtraction and percentage before selecting the rule branch; check the arithmetic with an available read-only calculation tool when one exists. A component included in the reported comprehensive total is not subtracted again, and a separate historical trial's margin does not establish that the current application is below the floor. If the current-project scope, total-cost meaning, included costs or first-curve classification is unresolved, do not infer a special-approval or earn-back obligation from the trial row; resolve those facts first. Reaching 25% does not by itself make the case approvable; the rest of the checklist still applies.
3. **Below 25% requires the form's special-approval path and proactive business-value review** (Derek, 2026-09-23):
   - The applicant must check the form's **"特批"** option when a first-curve project's comprehensive gross margin is below 25%. If the box is not checked, **return the application** for the applicant to select it; do not reject it for the margin shortfall and do not select the box on the applicant's behalf.
   - A checked request must include either a business-value assessment or a plan for how the project will earn back the margin shortfall. If neither is supplied, return it for that applicant-provided material. Do not treat the checkbox or applicant's conclusion as proof that the exception is justified.
   - Independently assess the case using the evidence actually available: strategic fit and market/customer importance; confirmed customer commitment, revenue and budget; product or algorithm improvement and measurable efficiency gain; reusable data, capability or delivery assets; total effort/opportunity cost; and whether risks and delivery boundaries are controllable. Separate verified facts from applicant claims. Do not invent a numeric business-value cutoff that this Skill does not define.
   - If the evidence supports a compelling, strategically relevant case and the material facts are complete, state **"recommend special approval"**, explain the value, trade-offs and/or credible earn-back path, and identify the required special approver/manager decision. Execute the exception only when the current authorization explicitly permits it and the authorized special approver's decision is recorded; otherwise route that approval decision to `needs_human`. Proactive assessment is not itself authority to grant an exception.
   - If the value case or earn-back path cannot be assessed because applicant-supplied facts are missing (for example, revenue/customer commitment, cost assumptions, scope, measurable outcome or risk mitigation), **ask back / return for those specific facts**. The required form checkbox is a procedural gate, not a substitute for the reviewer assessing business value. Reassess the exception on resubmission.
   - If the needed company policy, exception boundary, special-approver identity/authority is missing or conflicting, or complete facts still leave a material strategic trade-off that the reviewer is not authorized to decide, set `rule_coverage < 1.0` where the rule is missing and route to `needs_human`. Do not ask the applicant to create company policy or decide the company's strategic trade-off.
   - If the evidence is complete and affirmatively shows no material strategic/customer/product/algorithm/reusable value, or the benefits do not justify the 25% shortfall and risks, conclude that the exception is not justified and reject only when the generic Skill's rejection preconditions are met. If a curable factual gap remains, ask back instead.
4. **A low margin with no strategic, product, algorithm or reusable value** weighs against the exception; state the evidence and rationale. Mere absence of an applicant-authored exception request is not evidence that the business case lacks value.

Before rejecting, the generic Skill's reject preconditions still apply: check `revert-activities`, and if the reason asks the applicant to supply anything, it is a return, not a rejection.

## Not yet defined here

These block only a case that actually turns on them; when one does, keep `rule_coverage < 1.0`, name the exact branch, and escalate `needs_human`:

- who the "responsible manager" is for a given project and who holds final exception authority; the reviewer can still assess and state an exception recommendation without resolving this, but may not execute a grant until authority is clear;
- financial thresholds and boundaries for projects that are not first-curve;
- which version of the project-governance standard governs when v0.2 and v0.3 conflict on the point in question.

Actions follow the generic Skill's decision table. A project type not having its own action list is not a rule gap.

## Required review output

Include live process identity; classification and its evidence; each material read; cross-source scope and schedule discrepancies and their resolution state; the financial basis ledger (decision unit, period, revenue, designated comprehensive/total cost, component inclusion and separate historical or scenario rows); the chosen current-project margin subtraction and percentage and any separately labelled trial margin; for a confirmed below-25% first-curve project, whether the form's special-approval box is checked and whether the applicant supplied a business-value assessment or earn-back plan; the rule branch applied (rules 1–4 above); facts versus assertions; the reviewer's independent business-value assessment; `information_completeness`, `rule_coverage`, risk and confidence; recommendation; applicant question or return reason if any; and each undefined item above that the case depends on.
